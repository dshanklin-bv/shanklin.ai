What we need to buy

One long car, and everything it takes to keep it running. Concept

Planned · Fort Worth, Texas · Not operating yet. Stretchgood is a plan, not a running service. It is not giving rides, has no established 501(c)(3) status, and has no partner commitments. Nothing here can be booked or donated to, and no ride is promised.

This is the shopping list, not a receipt. We have not bought the car, and we will not treat a listing as a purchase.

The car

The target is a used stretch Town Car. Public listings we can point to right now include a 2005 at $7,500, a 2008 in Texas at $11,500, and a 2007 at $12,500Needs Daniel's check. Earlier shopping also turned up auction results in the mid-to-high thousandsNeeds Daniel's check, and a current candidate listed at $3,900 that has not been verified. The low price is interesting only if a mechanic says the car is sound.

Before any money changes hands we want:

  • A pre-purchase inspection, preferably by someone who has seen stretched cars, not only stock sedans. Typical Texas mobile inspections are listed around $129–$179Needs Daniel's check; a more thorough job may cost more.
  • A look at the frame, the stretch joint, the air-conditioning, the brakes, the tires, and the title history.
  • A clear plan for Texas title, registration, and 6.25% motor vehicle sales tax, including the possibility that a private-party sale is taxed on a higher standard presumptive valueNeeds Daniel's check.
  • A place to keep a very long car. An ordinary garage space will not do.

If someone offers to donate a vehicle, it goes through the same inspection. A gift that needs thousands of dollars of work is not automatically a gift we should take.

To get the car legal and usable

  • Texas title and registration, and an emissions test if the car is in the age window for Tarrant CountyNeeds Daniel's check.
  • Immediate repairs and tires, only as the inspection requires. We are not guessing those totals on this page.
  • Insurance, once we have a quote, with a coverage target of $500,000 combined single limit if the city's vehicle-for-hire rules applyNeeds Daniel's check. See Open decisions.
  • The city's $500 operating license, only if those rules apply to free rides.

To keep it running

  • Fuel. Stretch limousines are thirsty. Published operator figures cluster around 8–15 miles per gallonNeeds Daniel's check. At Texas's recent regular-gas average, even a modest first season is a real line item, which is why we would welcome in-kind fuel help.
  • Maintenance. An older stretched Town Car will cost more to keep than a new sedan. We have a published per-mile average for ordinary new cars, and we are treating it as a floor, not a forecast.
  • Storage. Covered or indoor space is kinder to the car; it also costs more.
  • Cleaning. The car has to feel special when someone steps in. That means regular cleaning, and professional detailing when a wipe-down is not enough.
  • Basic supplies: safety kit, phone charger, water, a way to keep the interior ready for a formal night.

To do the work carefully

  • Driver vetting, once we decide who drives: a driving-record check and a serious background check. A Texas fingerprint check for an unpaid role is listed at $38Needs Daniel's check, which is a starting point, not the whole safeguarding plan.
  • A simple way to schedule rides, keep notes, and, later, publish what each ride cost.
  • The legal paperwork for whichever structure we choose: a fiscal-sponsor agreement, or a Texas certificate of formation and an IRS application. Those filing fees are small. Doing them carefully matters more than the fee.

The website itself does not need a separate budget. It can live here, on shanklin.ai.

A fuller budget, with the math, is on the Financials page. If you have something on this list you can give or do, the Get involved page is the place.
