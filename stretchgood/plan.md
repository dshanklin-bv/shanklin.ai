# Plan · Stretchgood

> **V1 · figures are estimates.**

*Stretchgood · 03 / 08 · Planned · Fort Worth, Texas · Not operating yet*

Stretchgood is a plan, not a running service. It is not giving rides, has no established 501(c)(3) status, and has no partner commitments. Nothing here can be booked or donated to, and no ride is promised.

Items marked [Needs Daniel's check] are unverified.

This is the plan. Stretchgood is not operating yet. We have not bought a car, formed a charity, or taken anyone on a ride.

## What is settled

- The name is Stretchgood, and it's staying.
- This will be a Fort Worth, Texas effort.
- The rides will be completely free. No fares, no tips, no gas money, ever.
- We will start with prom kids who might not otherwise get that night.
- If we can do that well, later rides will be for hospice patients, veterans, and wish-style experiences. No affiliation with Make-A-Wish or any other wish-granting organization.
- The first ride is aimed at the spring 2027 prom season.
- The proposed first partner is a local prom-dress charity. We do not have an agreement yet, so we are not naming anyone.
- This page, at shanklin.ai/stretchgood, is where we keep the plan in the open.

## What we are working through

- We are shopping for a used stretch Town Car. A low-priced candidate has been listed, but it is unverified. We will not treat a listing price as a done deal.
- How we will be organized is still an open question: work under a fiscal sponsor, or form a Texas nonprofit and apply for federal tax-exempt status. We will not claim either status before it is real. See [Open decisions](/stretchgood/open-decisions/).
- Insurance, the city's vehicle-for-hire rules, and who drives are also not decided. Those questions are written out on the same page.
- The [Financials](/stretchgood/financials/) page is the working budget, with ranges, sources, and the holes we still have, including insurance, which has no quote yet.

## The sequence we have in mind

1. Finish the public plan and the budget you can see.
2. Choose a structure that lets people support the work without us pretending we already have tax-exempt status.
3. Get a written handle on insurance and the city question before anyone rides.
4. Decide who drives, and put vetting and safeguarding for minors in place before the first prom night.
5. Inspect and, only then, buy or accept a car.
6. Work with a local prom-dress charity on a first, small set of rides for spring 2027.
7. Publish what it actually cost, per ride, and what we would do differently.

If a step slips, we will say so here. A plan that cannot be revised in public is not a plan worth keeping.
