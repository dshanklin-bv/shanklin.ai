Financials

Every line counted. Nothing guessed. Concept

Planned · Fort Worth, Texas · Not operating yet. Stretchgood is a plan, not a running service. It is not giving rides, has no established 501(c)(3) status, and has no partner commitments. Nothing here can be booked or donated to, and no ride is promised.

We want Stretchgood's books to be as plain as the mission. Good small organizations publish a startup budget, an operating budget, and a cost per person they serve, and they label what is a real figure and what is still an estimate. That is what this page tries to do.

This is a working budget for a planned effort. We have not bought a car, raised a round of funding, or received an insurance quote. We will not invent a grand total that hides those holes. Every number below is either sourced, carried over from our own shopping notes, or marked as a planning assumption. The rides will be completely free (no fares, no tips, no gas money), so the budget has no passenger revenue.

When a figure changes, this page should change with it.

How to read this

  • Sourced means we can point to a public listing, a government fee, or a published rate.
  • Shopping notes means a figure from our own search for a car, not independently re-verified as a live listing here.
  • Planning assumption means we had to choose a reasonable range (for example, how many rides, or how many miles) in order to do the math. Those are not forecasts.
  • Unknown means we will not fill the gap with a guess.

First-year operating

The first rides are aimed at spring 2027 prom season. We do not know how many we will give. To show the math, we use three planning assumptions: 10, 25, or 50 rides, and 30, 50, or 80 miles per ride (including driving to the pickup and home again). Those mileages are assumptions, not logged trips.

Cost per ride

Estimate · built from the figures above, not a quote

We are showing two views, the way a careful nonprofit would. Both exclude insurance, because we do not have a quote. Both also use the mid operating subtotal of $2,634, which itself includes a maintenance number that is probably too low.

View A: year-one operating cost only (the cash it takes to give the ride, not counting the car):

  • 10 rides: $2,634 ÷ 10 = about $263 per ride
  • 25 rides: $2,634 ÷ 25 = about $105 per ride
  • 50 rides: $2,634 ÷ 50 = about $53 per ride

If actual operating costs land at the lean $1,077, those figures become about $108, $43, and $22. If they land at the higher $6,064, they become about $606, $243, and $121. Insurance would raise every one of these.

View B: same operating mid case, plus the car spread over five years. This is not depreciation for tax purposes. It is a way to see the all-in cost of the first season. We use a mid purchase in the sourced listing band: $10,000 + 6.25% tax ($625) + $130 for title, registration, and emissions + $150 inspection = $10,905 to get the car titled, before repairs. $10,905 ÷ 5 years = $2,181 per year.

  • 10 rides: ($2,634 + $2,181) ÷ 10 = about $482 per ride
  • 25 rides: ($2,634 + $2,181) ÷ 25 = about $193 per ride
  • 50 rides: ($2,634 + $2,181) ÷ 50 = about $96 per ride

If we somehow bought a sound car at the unverified $3,900 price, the five-year share would be much smaller (about $4,400 startup ÷ 5 ≈ $880 a year, or $35–$88 per ride on top of operating costs). We are not counting on that.

What we will not do is pick one of these numbers, strip the assumptions off it, and call it "the cost of a Stretchgood ride." After the first season, we want to replace this page with actual dollars and actual miles.

Overhead

Year one has no planned payroll. The main overhead we can already name is a fiscal sponsor's 5–10% fee, if we go that routeNeeds Daniel's check, plus the ordinary costs of keeping the car legal and clean. We will report program costs (the rides) separately from administration and fundraising once there is real money moving. Until there is, a program-versus-overhead ratio would be theater.

What this page is not

It is not an audit, not a Form 990, and not a promise that donations are tax-deductible. It is the honest math we have today. When we have better numbers, especially an insurance quote and a mechanic's report, they will go here.
