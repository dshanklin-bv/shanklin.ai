Fundraising

Paid for by people who show up. Never by the riders.

Planned · Fort Worth, Texas · Not operating yet. Stretchgood is a plan, not a running service. It is not giving rides, has no established 501(c)(3) status, and has no partner commitments. Nothing here can be booked or donated to, and no ride is promised.

Highlighted figures and rules are marked like thisNeeds Daniel's check until they are verified.

The rides will always be free to the people in the car. The money has to come from somewhere else. This page is the plan for that, written before we have a structure that can accept tax-deductible gifts. We will not pretend we already do.

Excellent small nonprofits tend to do a few things well: they ask clearly, they show the math, they make it easy to give things other than cash, and they use the local rails that already exist. We want Stretchgood to follow that pattern.

First decision: how gifts can be received

There are two honest paths. We have not chosen one.

A fiscal sponsor. An existing 501(c)(3) can receive tax-deductible gifts for a project like this, keep legal control of the funds, and charge a fee for the service. Published guidance commonly puts that fee at about 5–10% of the money receivedNeeds Daniel's check, sometimes a little more when the sponsor does more of the back office. This is often the faster way for a new project to raise money without building a whole charity first.

Our own nonprofit. We could form a Texas nonprofit corporation (the state filing fee is $25Needs Daniel's check) and apply to the IRS (the user fee is $275 on Form 1023-EZ, or $600 on Form 1023Needs Daniel's check, and the EZ form is only for organizations that meet the IRS size rules). Approval takes time. Until it is granted, we are not a 501(c)(3), and we will not say we are.

Some donors and foundations prefer to give to an organization that already has its own exemption. Some are comfortable giving through a sponsor. We will pick the path that gets the work started cleanly, then say so here.

Until one of those is in place, please do not send money with the expectation of a tax deduction. If you want to help anyway, start with the Get involved page.

Where the money can come from

People. Individual gifts are how a lot of small, local efforts actually start: a few people who care about the night, and who can see what the money buys. We want a public cost-per-ride figure, once we have real costs, so a gift can be understood in plain terms: this is what it takes to take someone to prom.

In-kind gifts. This may matter as much as cash. A donated or deeply discounted stretch limo, a safe place to park a long car, fuel, tires, inspection labor, or regular detailing would come straight off the budget. Prom-dress charities and honor-flight groups often run on this kind of help. We would need to inspect a donated vehicle with the same care as a purchased one, and we would need to handle the title and tax rules correctly.

Local businesses. A shop, a dealer, a detailer, or a company that wants to underwrite a season of rides, without turning the car into a rolling billboard that gets in the way of the night. Sponsorship should be public and specific: what was given, and what it covered.

Community foundations and small grants. North Texas Community Foundation, here in Fort Worth, makes competitive grants and also awards gifts that donors recommend from funds they already keep there.Needs Daniel's check We would need a qualified nonprofit or a fiscal sponsor to be eligible, and we would apply only for funds that actually fit. Communities Foundation of Texas is the other large regional foundation in this picture, and it is the home of North Texas Giving Day.Needs Daniel's check

North Texas Giving Day and other giving days. North Texas Giving Day is the region's annual community giving event, usually in September. For 2027, registration is already being offered, and the organizers say participating nonprofits receive 100% of the gifts, with transaction fees covered.Needs Daniel's check That only helps us once we have a qualified organization or sponsor to register. Giving Tuesday and year-end giving can use the same rails.

Workplace matching. Many employers match employee gifts to 501(c)(3) organizations. That is a reason to get the structure right, not a source we can claim today.

Small grants and churches, civic clubs, and school communities. Honor Flight hubs often ask civic groups to underwrite a veteran.Needs Daniel's check A prom ride is a smaller ask, and a natural fit for people who already care about the kids in their schools. We will only pursue this once we can receive the money properly.

How we want to ask

Show the budget. Show the cost per ride once we can. Say what a gift will buy. Publish what came in and what it paid for. Do not dress up overhead, and do not hide the fact that insurance and repairs are still open. If a funder needs a Form 990 or an audit, we will build toward that when the size of the work requires it, not before.

The Financials page is the beginning of that habit.
