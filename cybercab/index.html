Buying a Tesla Cybercab

I'm replacing my car, and I'm looking at the Tesla Cybercab. This page lists the steps to buy one, what I've learned, and where I am.

Not legal, insurance, or tax advice. Researched November 16, 2026.

Tesla hosts the Cybercab Autonomy Subscription Agreement (version 20261112) at a tesla.com configurator URL. I still need to open it in a normal browser and save it before I pay a deposit. One outlet links the Order Agreement at a matching address; that was not confirmed. Where a fact below comes from an outlet's summary of an agreement, rather than Tesla's own text, I write "reportedly." (Wonderful World of EVs, Nov 14 · Electrek, Nov 14)

Where I am: I submitted the interest form on Nov 16, 2026, and have no invite yet.

What I've found: It's a two-seat car that only runs inside the Robotaxi zone, the owner reportedly has to be in it for every trip, and a hybrid costs less.

What would stop me: Insurance (no insurer has committed yet, and the policy must reportedly cover trips with no one in the car), delivery city, and charging.

Background

In 1991, at age 7, I flew a Cessna 172 from San Diego to Kill Devil Hills, North Carolina, with my instructor, Stuart Moon. Then I was on Late Night with David Letterman.

Steps to buy one

The purchase price is $29,990, before taxes. The deposit is $1,000 and is non-refundable. The Autonomy Subscription is $299 a month, pre-checked on the order page, and required to operate. The first month is included. The page says: "Subscription and regular vehicle maintenance required to operate."

Sources: Electrek, Nov 13 · The Verge, Nov 14 · Not a Tesla App, Nov 14

Invites went out Friday, Nov 13, 2026. Tesla's Robotaxi account said "select customers are now being invited to purchase Cybercab for personal use." Reports say the invitation calls it an "Early Access, Limited Release" and says initial availability is limited. Estimated delivery is December 2026. That window is an estimate and is not guaranteed.

Sources: Electrek, Nov 13 · ev.guru, Nov 15 · The Verge, Nov 14

Reports on the order page say the limit is one Cybercab per buyer. The car has two seats, no steering wheel, and no pedals. The invitation lists a 21-inch center touchscreen and 20.1 cubic feet of storage.

Sources: Teslarati, Nov 15 · Digital Trends, Nov 14 · Electrek, Nov 13 · Tesla Robotaxi

Personal use only. Reports say owners may not put the car on Tesla's Robotaxi network, or on Uber, delivery, or rental platforms, unless Tesla authorizes it in writing or enables it in the product. Tesla AI chief Ashok Elluswamy has said customer cars might be allowed to join the Robotaxi network around Q2 2027. That is a target, not a commitment.

Sources: The Verge, Nov 14 · TeslaNorth, Nov 13 · Electrek, Nov 14

If the $299 price does not change, the car plus 59 paid months of the subscription is $47,631, before tax, registration, insurance, charging, and parking. Three years is $40,455. Tesla can change the subscription price with one month's notice, so these totals can change. Elon Musk said on X that the $299 fee is "too high" and should be closer to about $115 a month. That is a comment, not a price change.

Sources: ev.guru, Nov 15 · Electrek, Nov 14 · TechCrunch, Nov 14

Texas motor vehicle sales tax is 6.25% of the sales price, minus any trade-in. On $29,990 with no trade-in, that is $1,874.38, before any other fees. (Texas Comptroller)

Interest form

Tesla's public form is at tesla.com/robotaxi/interest. I submitted it on Nov 16, 2026, for a personal purchase in Fort Worth. The page says: "Complete our interest form to be considered for future Robotaxi opportunities." Unknown: whether this form is how Tesla chooses personal-purchase invitees, how it selects "select customers," how many invites it sent, or when orders will open to everyone.

Sources: Tesla Robotaxi · Tesla interest form · ev.guru, Nov 15 · The Verge, Nov 14

Invite, deposit, and order

Without an invitation, I cannot order. Tesla has not issued a press release or opened a public order page. Reports say the invitation's fine print is: "Only available in supported service areas. Subscription required for operation." By placing an order, a buyer agrees to the Cybercab Order Agreement, Terms of Use, Autonomy Subscription Agreement, and Privacy Notice. Invited buyers place the $1,000 non-refundable deposit on Tesla's order page.

Sources: Electrek, Nov 13 · TeslaNorth, Nov 13 · ev.guru, Nov 15 · The Verge, Nov 14

Delivery

Reports still conflict on whether Fort Worth delivery is offered. An invited buyer's post, plus Teslarati, EVwire, and Not a Tesla App, list five Texas cities: Austin, Dallas, Fort Worth, Houston, and San Antonio. Tesla's own invite screenshot, Teslarati's Nov 13 article, and Tesla Oracle show or describe Austin only. My read is that Fort Worth is offered, but I still have to see it on my own order page. Musk said the car "will be available nationwide, as we receive regulatory approval." That is a future claim, not current delivery policy.

Sources: MKBHD on X, Nov 14 · Teslarati, Nov 15 · EVwire, Nov 16 · Not a Tesla App, Nov 14 · Teslarati, Nov 13 (Austin only) · Tesla Oracle, Nov 14 (Austin only) · TechCrunch, Nov 14

Unknown: the exact pickup process for a Fort Worth-area buyer (service center, home drop-off, or something else). Tesla's usual Texas new-car process is that Tesla handles titling and registration after delivery and payment clear, mails registration and plates, and the state mails the title, with a temporary permit in the meantime. Whether Cybercab uses that same process is not confirmed in a Cybercab-specific document found for this page.

Sources: Tesla, Texas Vehicle Pick Up Process · Tesla Support, Registering Your Tesla

What I can do about it

If the live order page offers Fort Worth, I should choose Fort Worth. There is a Tesla service center in Fort Worth. Whether that lot is inside the usable geofence, and whether Cybercab deliveries happen there, must be confirmed with Tesla. (Tesla Find Us)

If delivery is Austin-only, the car almost certainly cannot drive itself from Austin to Fort Worth. The two Robotaxi zones do not connect, and reports say operation outside the enabled area may be technologically prevented. Tesla's usual paid home-delivery option is for customers who live more than 220 miles from the nearest Tesla delivery location. Austin to Fort Worth is about 180-190 driving miles, which is under that threshold, so this option may not be offered. Tesla has not published a Cybercab-specific transport fee. (ai.cars, Nov 6; TeslaNorth, Nov 13; Tesla Support, Taking Delivery)

A private enclosed car shipper for Austin to Fort Worth is commonly quoted at about $655-$860 for a standard car, 1-3 days in transit. Those quotes assume a normal car that can be driven onto a trailer. A Cybercab has no steering wheel or pedals, so a carrier may refuse it unless Tesla loads it. Do not treat that price range as a confirmed Cybercab shipping rate. (AutoShipCost; udecideautocarriers)

If the page is Austin-only, the workable fallback is to go to Austin for the delivery appointment and require Tesla in writing, before the deposit, to transport the car to Fort Worth and to state the fee. I should not plan to drive it back. If Tesla will not transport it, I should ask Tesla to load it for a carrier I hire, and get that in writing too.

Registration and title in Texas

Texas law allows an automated motor vehicle to operate with the automated driving system engaged, with or without a human driver present, if it meets the conditions in Transportation Code §545.455, including being registered and titled under Texas law and carrying required liability coverage or self-insurance. A privately owned automated vehicle used only for personal use does not need TxDMV's commercial automated-vehicle authorization. Title application and motor-vehicle tax are filed with the county tax assessor-collector on Form 130-U, generally within 30 days of purchase.

Sources: Transportation Code §545.455 · SB 2807 · TxDMV AV program · TxDMV law-enforcement handout, May 11, 2026 · Texas Comptroller · TxDMV Form 130-U

Unknown: whether Tesla will complete Form 130-U for a Cybercab the same way it does for other Teslas; whether Texas has any special title or plate notation for a vehicle with no steering wheel or pedals; and whether a Fort Worth-area buyer taking delivery in another Texas city has any extra steps.

Subscription

Reports say: "Your Cybercab will not operate without an active Autonomy Subscription." A Tesla Account and a compatible mobile device are required. Owners set destinations, manage trips and access, schedule service, and get support through Tesla's app. The subscription renews monthly and can be canceled at any time through the Tesla Account. Payments are not prorated. The first month is included with purchase. After that it is billed separately, excluding taxes.

Sources: Electrek, Nov 14 · Not a Tesla App, Nov 14 · TeslaNorth, Nov 13 · The Verge, Nov 14

Unknown: the exact activation steps at delivery (who starts billing, whether the first month starts on delivery day, and what I would have to tap in the app).

What works and what doesn't

  • Works: it drives itself inside Robotaxi zones, with no steering wheel or pedals.
  • Doesn't: it seats two, so it can't carry me and both kids. It can't leave the zones. It can't drive without the subscription. Owners can't use it commercially for now. It will not make a road trip to North Carolina, or a ski trip outside the zone. On current maps, it will not go to DFW Airport, Love Field, or Meacham, or drive from Fort Worth to Dallas in one trip.

No privately owned Cybercab had been delivered as of Nov 16, 2026. Estimated first deliveries are December 2026. The points below come from Tesla's Robotaxi service, journalist rides, and Tesla's own service documents, not from private owners.

The Verge spent about an hour in a Cybercab in Austin in early September. Wait times for short trips were long (about 45, 40, and 25 minutes on three rides). Fares were about $15–$21, similar to a base Uber. The ride was generally smooth and comfortable, with more legroom than a typical car because there is no wheel or pedals. The software sometimes felt hesitant. These are early Austin rides, not a Fort Worth-area owner's daily use, and not a reliability study. (The Verge, Sept 9, 2026)

Tesla has not published Cybercab-specific crash rates, remote-assistance rates, or miles with passengers. Unknown: how often a privately owned car would get stuck, need remote help, or miss a trip.

Weather

Tesla's Cybercab First Responder Interaction Plan (v1.2, Sept 3, 2026) says the car can operate day and night, and in light and moderate rain, fog, and snow. It is limited, and "not intended for use," in extreme weather: flooding, heavy rain, snowy or icy roads, and hurricane-strength winds. It is not designed to operate off-road. It does not accept new rides when extreme weather is expected. If caught in unexpected extreme weather, it is designed to pull over at the nearest safe stop and park. Tesla can also order vehicles to avoid affected roads or return to a charging station or parking lot when public safety alerts are issued. Reports say the subscription agreement also lets Tesla restrict operation by weather or condition. Unknown: how often Fort Worth-area storms would actually stop an owned car, and whether an owned car follows the same "no new rides" weather policy as the fleet.

Sources: FRIP PDF · TeslaNorth, Nov 13

Highways

The same plan says the operational design domain includes public roadways such as freeways, highways, city streets, and rural roads, plus private roads and service stations such as parking lots. How often public rides actually use highways, and whether every highway inside a zone is enabled, is not published. (FRIP PDF)

Kids and passengers

Two different rules appear in sources. They may apply to different products (Robotaxi rides versus an owned car). Both are reported. I still need to check the live agreements.

Robotaxi / Cybercab rider terms from September 2026, when public Cybercab rides began in Austin: children under 13 cannot ride in a Cybercab "at this time." A person must be 18 to request a ride or travel alone. All children under 18 must be accompanied by an adult, in both Cybercab and Model Y Robotaxi. Cybercab has no LATCH anchors. A child seat can only be attached with the seat belt. (TechCrunch, Sept 4, 2026)

Reports on the owned-car subscription agreement say only two people can ride at once, and that "Tesla advises against children under 13 riding, and any child who does must be accompanied by an adult and use the proper car seat." Those two wordings are not the same. One is a ban for Robotaxi Cybercab rides. The other is advice plus adult-and-car-seat conditions for an owned car. Unknown which rule I would actually have to follow. (Not a Tesla App, Nov 14)

Other cabin limits reported from Tesla's September Cybercab documents: windows "cannot be fully opened at this time." The car is not a roll-on wheelchair vehicle. Tesla says the trunk can hold a small folded wheelchair and there is room for a service animal. Other animals were prohibited on Robotaxi.

Sources: TechCrunch, Sept 4 · Tesla Robotaxi

Unknown: whether "supported service areas" for an owner will stay as large as today's Robotaxi maps, shrink, or grow. Reports say the contract lets Tesla change the area.

What I can do about it

The two-seat limit is a design limit, not a setting I can change. (Tesla Robotaxi) I should ask Tesla, in writing, which age rule applies to an owned car, and follow the stricter rule until I have a written answer. If a child is under 13, I should not plan on the Cybercab for that child.

The practical plan is to treat the Cybercab as a two-person, in-zone Fort Worth car and pair it with a regular family car of at least five seats, for trips with both kids, for DFW, Dallas, and nearby suburbs, and for road trips and ski trips. My Honda Pilot goes to my son, so a Cybercab cannot be the only remaining car if those trips still need to happen.

For in-zone trips of three or four people, I can hail a Model Y Robotaxi in the app when one is available. It seats up to four. Under the September Robotaxi rules, children 8-17 may ride with an adult; younger children need a child seat and may or may not be allowed. I should check the live rider rules. Fort Worth currently runs Model Y robotaxis. (Tesla Robotaxi; Not a Tesla App, Nov 12)

For airport and out-of-zone trips on days the family car is not available, I would use a rental, Uber, or similar. I should not count on putting the Cybercab on Tesla's Robotaxi network later to make up for a second car. Commercial use is barred unless Tesla later allows it. The earliest public target mentioned for customer cars joining the network is around Q2 2027, and that is not a promise. (The Verge, Nov 14)

Before a deposit, I should treat the Cybercab as a second, in-zone two-seater, not a Pilot replacement, and keep looking for a family car for the trips this car cannot do.

Insurance

For a privately owned Cybercab used for personal use, the owner is responsible for insurance. Tesla says the Autonomy Subscription does not include auto insurance. Owners also pay registration, charging, parking, and general cleaning.

Sources: Not a Tesla App, Nov 14 · The Verge, Nov 14 · TeslaNorth, Nov 13

Texas law requires motor vehicle liability coverage, or self-insurance, at least equal to the amount required for the type and use of the vehicle, for an automated motor vehicle operating with the driving system engaged. Texas's standard minimum auto liability is 30/60/25: $30,000 bodily injury per person, $60,000 bodily injury per accident, and $25,000 property damage per accident. The Texas Department of Insurance recommends considering higher limits. Unknown: whether any insurer or Tesla will require higher limits specifically for a driverless Cybercab. No public Cybercab insurance quotes were found.

Sources: Transportation Code §545.455 · TDI auto insurance guide

Tesla Insurance is offered in Texas, according to a state list compiled from Tesla's "What is Tesla Insurance" support page. Tesla's own page could not be loaded for this research on Nov 16, 2026 (it returned Access Denied), so the current list should be confirmed at tesla.com/support/insurance/what-is-tesla-insurance. Secondary compilation: Tesla Cost Tracker.

The Verge reported that Tesla has not explained how personal auto insurers will underwrite a car with no human driver, or how liability is split between the owner and Tesla if the vehicle causes an accident while operating autonomously. (The Verge, Nov 14)

The order agreement reportedly requires proof of insurance at delivery if the car will use public roads. The coverage must extend to unoccupied trips for charging, upkeep, and service. Tesla reportedly does not guarantee that insurance will be available. No insurer, Tesla Insurance included, has said publicly that it will cover a privately owned Cybercab.

Sources: EVwire, Nov 16 · Wonderful World of EVs, Nov 14

Fleet Robotaxi vehicles owned by Tesla show registration and proof of insurance at tesla.com/support/robotaxi/vehicle-info. That page is for Tesla's own fleet, not for a private owner. (Cybercab First Responder Interaction Plan, v1.2, Sept 3, 2026)

Texas Transportation Code §545.454, as amended by SB 2807, says that when the automated driving system is engaged, it is the operator of the vehicle, including for traffic-law compliance, and that citations go to the owner. The bill does not allocate civil liability. Reports say owners remain responsible for tolls, parking, and traffic violations. If the owner thinks the Cybercab caused a violation, they can submit documentation and ask Tesla to pay. Who pays after a crash is not settled in a public Tesla document found for this page.

Sources: Transportation Code §545.454 · Not a Tesla App, Nov 14 · Automotive World, Nov 16

What I can do about it

No insurer was found that has publicly said it will bind a personal auto policy on a privately owned Cybercab in Texas. That is still the gap. Tesla reportedly also requires that the policy cover trips with no one in the car. I should get a yes or no in writing before the $1,000 deposit, starting with Tesla Insurance, then a Texas agent who can shop other carriers.

Tesla Insurance quotes are requested in the Tesla app, or through Tesla Insurance. Tesla Insurance has, in its Texas program, offered an "Autonomous Vehicle Protection Package." That filing is from 2021 and was written for ordinary Tesla cars, not a Cybercab with no manual controls. It is a reason to ask Tesla Insurance first, not proof they will cover this car. (Insurance Journal, Feb 5, 2021; Tesla Support, How to Get Tesla Insurance; tesla.com/insurance)

Lemonade sells regular car insurance in Texas, but its "Autonomous" / FSD product is listed as "not available in Texas yet," and that product is a discount for supervised Full Self-Driving miles on a normal Tesla, not a policy for a driverless two-seater. I should not treat Lemonade as a Cybercab solution. (Lemonade availability; Lemonade on autonomous car insurance)

Koop has talked about insurance for autonomous fleets and a future product for personally owned robotaxis. No source found shows a Koop personal Cybercab policy I can buy in Texas today. (The Road to Autonomy transcript)

If Tesla Insurance or a standard Texas auto writer says no, the next step is an independent Texas agent who can ask surplus or specialty markets. That may take longer than the invite window. I should not pay the non-refundable deposit until at least one carrier has said they will bind, or Tesla has put in writing how insurance will be handled at delivery.

I can send Tesla Insurance a written list of questions myself, before any deposit: whether they will write a personal policy on a privately owned Cybercab in Texas; when they can bind; what limits they require; whether the policy covers the car while the automated system is engaged and no human is driving; who is treated as the operator; what happens if a crash is caused by Tesla's software; whether collision, comprehensive, uninsured/underinsured motorist, and personal injury protection are offered; exclusions for no manual controls, geofenced operation, unoccupied trips, or remote operation by Tesla; and whether comprehensive still applies if I cancel the subscription and the car is parked.

Charging

The order agreement reportedly says the car is compatible only with NACS DC fast charging, including Superchargers, and that the owner pays for charging. That means no onboard AC charger, and a Tesla Wall Connector or Mobile Connector will not work. The car takes DC through a NACS port behind an access panel in the rear bumper. Tesla has not published a consumer charging guide for a privately owned Cybercab. I should still read the clause on the live order page.

Sources: EVwire, Nov 16, 2026 · Wonderful World of EVs, Nov 14 · The Gadgeteer, Nov 14 · InsideEVs, Sept 11, 2026

A conflicting detail: Cybercab EPA certification documents (test group TTSLV00.0L1A, certified May 26, 2026) list "Recharge energy (AC from wall): 53.365 kWh." That figure implies some AC charging path was used in the EPA test. It does not, by itself, prove a production consumer car has a usable home AC port. (Electrek, June 15, 2026)

That settles home charging with a Wall Connector: it will not work. No Tesla home DC or wireless product for owners was found. The EPA figure does not, by itself, prove a production consumer car has a usable home AC port.

Tesla's long-term plan is inductive charging: the car parks over a ground pad. The FCC granted Tesla a waiver to use ultra-wideband radio to align the vehicle with the pad. Tesla has said efficiency is well above 90%. An older demonstration showed charging at up to about 25 kW. With a battery under 50 kWh, InsideEVs estimated a full wireless charge could take around two hours if that rate holds. Tesla has discussed a private Austin Robotaxi hub with 80 wireless chargers. That is fleet infrastructure, not a confirmed home product. No source found for this page shows a Tesla home wireless charging pad on sale.

Sources: electrive, Feb 21, 2026 · Not a Tesla App, Oct 12 · InsideEVs, Sept 11

Fleet Cybercabs use public Superchargers. A video from early October 2026 showed a driverless Cybercab and Model Y robotaxis parking themselves at a public Supercharger in Austin while a Tesla employee plugged each one in. The Cybercab reversed in because the NACS port is in the rear bumper. The subscription does not include charging cost.

Sources: Not a Tesla App, Oct 12 · Not a Tesla App, Nov 14

The order agreement reportedly requires the registered owner to be physically present for every trip. Exceptions are family sharing in the app, and unoccupied trips for Tesla features or subscription services, only as far as Tesla enables them. The same reports say the owner's insurance must cover unoccupied trips for charging, upkeep, and service. No Tesla statement was found saying unoccupied charging trips are switched on for owner cars at delivery. Still unknown: whether Supercharger access is billed to the Tesla Account or limited to certain stalls, and whether Fort Worth or Dallas has a Robotaxi-only charging hub a private owner can use.

Sources: EVwire, Nov 16 · Wonderful World of EVs, Nov 14 · The Gadgeteer, Nov 14

Tesla's First Responder Interaction Plan says a Cybercab Robotaxi, while waiting for a booking, roams its area or goes to a charging station or parking lot, and goes to a charger if the battery is too low for another ride. As of October 2026, the actual plug-in at public Superchargers was still done by a person. Wireless hubs were not yet the normal method.

Sources: FRIP v1.2, Sept 3, 2026 · Not a Tesla App, Oct 12

For context, not as a charging method: EPA certification lists a lithium-ion pack of about 47.6 kWh and a curb weight of 3,113 lbs. Electrek translated the unadjusted combined range to an adjusted figure of about 293 miles. Tesla had previously said "close to 300 miles." (Electrek, June 15, 2026)

What I can do about it

Two public Tesla Superchargers sit in parts of Fort Worth already listed as covered: the University Park Village V4 site (16 stalls, up to 325 kW, open 24 hours) and another nearby Supercharger (16 stalls; DCFC Tracker listed peak power at 75 kW on Nov 16, 2026, and some directories still list 150 kW). Both are very likely inside the Fort Worth zone, because they sit in the Cultural District and by TCU, which independent maps list as covered. I still need to confirm both as valid destinations in the Tesla or Robotaxi app. (DCFC Tracker, University Park Village, rates dated Nov 16, 2026; DCFC Tracker, nearby Supercharger, rates dated Nov 16, 2026; tesla-chargers.com; PlugShare, nearby Supercharger; Not a Tesla App, Nov 5)

Some farther Superchargers may or may not be in the zone. I should not count on them until the app shows them as a legal destination. No Fort Worth Robotaxi-only charging hub was found. Private Dallas fleet stalls and a planned Irving hub are outside the Fort Worth zone. Irving sits between the Dallas and Fort Worth geofences. (Road to Autonomy DFW field report; EVwire on the Irving hub; Fort Worth Report, Nov 2)

What is confirmed for Tesla's own fleet, not for a private owner: the car can drive itself, empty, to a charging site, but at public Superchargers a Tesla employee still plugs the cable in. The workable method today, until Tesla confirms otherwise: ride with the car to University Park Village or another nearby Supercharger, plug in the NACS cable myself, wait through the charge or stay nearby, unplug, and ride back. No Tesla home wireless pad, and no consumer sale date, was found as of Nov 16, 2026. A Wall Connector will not work if the production car has no onboard AC charger.
